I think we all think of humility as a good thing, in the sense that it serves as the opposite of acting like a massive self-obsessed jerk. But I often think about humility and how it seems to be a concept that is interpreted and expressed in a number of ways, some of which seem to be mutually contradictory. 

One definition seems to be simply about not showing off about something. A humble person who has achieved something doesn't make a big fuss about their achievements, perhaps out of consideration for people who are part of the conversation who haven't been as successful. This seems like a valid reason for being humble and one that should be encouraged. 

But the concept of humility becomes a bit more problematic when it's not so much a act of private behaviour as it is a public declaration. Chief among these is the thing that a lot of people say when they have a big success, and how they are humbled by it. 

Again, I get where this comes from. You can't just start bragging when you win an award, you need to be respectful towards the other nominees while at the same time seeming like they deserve to have won. Deep down, the theatre of the awards ceremony demands that the winner is also a good person so that participants can continue to believe that (in direct opposition to Nietzsche) we are rewarded for our virtues. I also believe that there are many cultures where people are brought up to seek success while at the same time remaining visibly humble despite all the contradictions and cognitive dissonance that this involves.
