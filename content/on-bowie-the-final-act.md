I just watched Bowie: The FInal Act on the Channel 4 online page. Out of the documentaries made since Bowie's Death, with the BBC 5 Years docs and Moonage Daydream, this was without doubt the weakest. Not because of the over-reliance on Talking Heads that made it feel more like your standard TV filler, but also in how it flattened and simplified the output of Bowie's final decades too. 

Those decades might have been inconsistent, and some of the work was derivative of whatever was happening at the time, but there was also plenty of good work put out there. It seems to be that Blackstar being his only great album since Scary Monsters is the new Heathen being the only great album since Scary Monsters. But even then, if you put the whole album thing aside and just look at the tracks, you find plenty that stand up and often exceed the middling tracks from some of those classic 70s LPs. 

I'm Deranged, Halo Spaceboy, Survive, Something in the Air, Afraid, Monday. I'll even go out on a limb and say that Little Wonder has aged particularly well, in the sense that the shock of it's drum'n'bass percussion has subsided and it can be seen for what it always has been, a decent, catchy Bowie song with some drum'n'bass elements. New Killer Star, the opening track of Reality, the album he released before the heart attack triggered the end of touring and a decade's hiatus, felt more derivative, being that felt more like a copy paste of Blur's Coffee and TV (though the video was fantastic). But that same album also contained Bring Me the Disco King
