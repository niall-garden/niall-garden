While I'm aware that there's a lot of discourse about taking photos of or filming wrestlers in hotel lobbies or airports, I can't help but remember seeing a picture of the Undertaker sat down in an airport in 2014, not long after he lost the streak to Lesnar at Wrestlemania 30. He'd grown a grey goattee, and short grey hair was clearly visible underneath his baseball cap. Some fans [reacted in horror](https://www.kayfabenews.com/wrestling-fans-shocked-undertaker-ages-normally/) to the sight of a 48-year-old man with grey hair. Others, such as yours truly, felt different.

The first thing I thought was that it would be incredibly cool to have an ageing, grey haired Undertaker leaning even harder into the Western elements of his gimmick. Unforgiven is still one of my top-five Westerns. Logan is still one of my top-five superhero movies. I love the story of the old gunslinger not losing his edge in his autumn years. With the streak gone, there was still the prospect of a story to be told. Whether Mark Callous could still be mean as the sun set on his career.

Of course, that never happened. This was the same company who took wrestling's preeminent silver fox, Kevin Nash, and had him walk out for WWE with a Just-For-Men sheen to his hair, giving CM Punk all the ammunition he needed to utter a line that still seems to echo within Nash's head [to this day](https://www.cagesideseats.com/wwe/408736/kevin-nash-to-cm-punk-easier-to-go-to-gym). Similarly, Taker continued to slow walk to the ring without a strand of grey showing, sometimes looking like he had glued the fake locks from Kane's mask to his pate. We can of course infer the reason why we never got the Old-Man-Taker angle. It probably had more to do with [a certain old man](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AG5thBg9Mu4) with a marked disdain for the realities of age than it had with Calloway, who [brought out the grey](https://www.cagesideseats.com/wwe/390342/wwe-president-nick-khan-expects-fans-travel-saudi-arabia-wrestlemania-43-2027-international) one more time at the recent Saudi Wrestlemania announcement.

This is one reason why I don't consider this year's Wrestlemania to be a complete write off. Amidst the barrage of advertising, the rushed matches and hurried entrances –⁠ there was still room for a motif to subtly repeat itself, and that was very much the theme of one generation passing the torch to another.

One element seemed to be how one talent recognised their own twilight with grace while another raged against the dying of the light. Brock Lesnar was never one to draw out the end of his career as a gatekeeper, and I personally believe that the gloves, boots and crossed wrists signalled that the Beast was picking his own moment to say goodbye. On the other end of things we had Randy Orton, wincing with back pain throughout his bout and coming up short at the end of it, only to fly into such a rage that he coshed his young usurper over the head with the WWE championship and topped it with a punt for good measure. I think we would all gratefully take a similar boot to the head if it dislodged all recollection of McAfee's involvement.

And then there was Punk. A man who seems just as averse to hair dye as he is to alcohol and drugs, yet still loses his (kayfabe) rag when being addressed by Roman Reigns as, "old". These two consummate storytellers were never going to have a bad match and I loved how the main event followed a particular hook that we've seen in previous Manias –⁠ the one where a wrestler isn't taken out by a particular power move or third finisher in a row, but runs out of gas instead. The will is still there but the body can no longer carry it out.

We saw it when Flair stood in the centre of the ring and raised his fists as Shawn Michaels tuned up the band. We saw it when Michaels could just about summon the strength for one last slap across the Dead Man's jaw before the curtains closed on the Showstopper. And we saw it again when Punk hoisted Reigns up to his shoulders for one more GTS before his legs buckled. This was followed by a final attempt at a slap that, unlike Michaels, swished well clear of its target before Reigns hit the spear one more decisive time. This particular storytelling beat never fails to catch me in the feels.

We might still get our wrestling version of the story of William Munny or Old Man Logan. Fifty has been the new forty for quite a while now but 48 is a ripe age in wrestler years. It was the age Taker was at when he raised a fist for that airport photograph and it's the age that Punk is at now. But Punk has always done his own thing, so I think we can safely say that there's no hair dye or hair extensions on his particular horizon. And nobody can rock a wig like Kurt Angle was able to.

But for all the critiques we have of Paul Levesque's booking, one strength has always been his focus on the long term. I don't think the "old" thing will be packed away at the culmination of this Mania feud. As a parade of upcoming big boys seems ready to make WWE the place where they play, there is still a compelling story to be told in the squared circle about the unremitting passage of time and Punk might just be the one to tell it.

It would also be remiss of me not to mention the elephant in the room. If the men have been under pressure not to tell the story about ageing, then it has been several orders of magnitude worse for the women. This is perhaps too much to get into in a final paragraph. I can only say that if, in ten year's time, I get the chance to to see a grey haired Asuka still kicking the young ladies' heads off their shoulders, I will be a very happy man indeed.
